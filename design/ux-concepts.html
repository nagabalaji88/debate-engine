Layout exploration · not an implementation

Six ways to show a machine changing its mind.

Every screen below renders the same debate: “Should the payments platform ship as a modular monolith or as microservices for the next 18 months?” — five models, one independent judge, outcome MAJORITY_WITH_DISSENT at 84% confidence. Same decision object, six different layout arguments about which part of it deserves the pixels.

Principle 1 renders as structure

Claims are first-class objects on screen — addressable IDs, evidence type, relationships. No layout aggregates models into a percentage bar.

Principle 2 renders as colour

Dissent gets its own semantic channel (brick) and its own real estate. A split decision must look as finished as a consensus, never like a failure state.

Principle 3 renders as a doorway

Every decision surface ends in one primary action — open Build Studio. The debate is never the last screen a user sees.

Layout BDocument-first · single column with margin apparatus

BDocket

The decision as a written opinion, with the machinery in the margin. One 680px measure of serif prose, every assertion carrying a clickable claim citation, and the dissent set as its own block rather than a footnote. This is the artefact that gets pasted into an RFC — so it has to survive being read by someone who never watched the debate.

Decision

Build the payments platform as a modular monolith with enforced module boundaries and a single deployable, and extract services only when a named scaling or compliance trigger fires.

Why

The strongest surviving evidence concerns operational load rather than architecture aesthetics. On-call burden scales with service count C-011 · fact, and the domains under discussion — settlement and ledger — scale together, so splitting them buys no independent scaling headroom C-018 · inference. The one compliance argument that survived cross-examination cuts the same way: isolating card data to a single deployable boundary reduces PCI scope C-002 · fact, and every model in the panel accepted it.

The case for microservices rested on a claim the panel could not substantiate — that a twelve-engineer team can operate six to eight services without dedicated platform staffing C-024 · assumption. Challenged directly, its author narrowed it to “with a platform team of at least three”, which the requirements do not fund.

Dissent

Two models declined to converge. Both argued that a regulated payments domain should default to service isolation regardless of team size C-006 · opinion. The judge scored the claim low on evidence quality (0.41) but high on risk awareness (0.79): the reasoning is unevidenced, but the risk it names — a blast radius spanning settlement and ledger in one deployable — is real and unaddressed by the recommendation.

This dissent is not resolved by this decision. It is a live constraint on the design that follows.

What would change this decision

Three triggers, each traceable to an unresolved claim: platform engineering headcount reaching 4 FTE C-024; a settlement latency SLO below 150ms that the shared runtime cannot hold C-031; or a regulatory requirement for physically separated card-data processing C-006. Until one fires, extraction is deferred, not rejected.

Screen 4 · Explore claims. Dimmed cards are agreed-and-uncontested — present but demoted. The matrix is the only place model identity is foregrounded, and even there the row, not the column, is the subject.

Why it works

Answers “where do they actually disagree?” in one glance, and shows that most of a debate is agreement — which is what makes the dissent meaningful.

Watch out

Thirty-two cards is a wall. Default to critical disputes plus top-evidence agreed claims, with “show all” as an explicit act.

Cheap version

Ship the matrix before the map. The graph needs layout work; the table needs one query and already carries the same information.

Layout DOne thing per screen · mobile-first, scales up

DFocus Flow

The same six screens with everything deferred. You ask, you pick a panel, you watch a stepper rather than a stream, you get a decision card. Claims live one tap down. It’s the version you can hand to someone who has never heard the phrase “cross-examination” — and the only one that works while a debate runs for three minutes in your pocket.

# Build: Payments Platform — Modular Monolith

## Context
You are implementing a payments platform chosen by a five-model
deliberation (outcome: MAJORITY_WITH_DISSENT, confidence 84).
Two models dissented; their risk is binding, not advisory.

## Non-negotiable constraints
1. One deployable. Modules communicate through published
   interfaces only — no cross-module table access. (C-018)
2. Card data confined to a single module boundary for PCI
   scope reduction. (C-002)
3. Module-level circuit breakers + a settlement kill switch,
   because blast radius spans settlement and ledger. (dissent)
4. CI enforces boundaries: an import-graph check fails the
   build on any illegal edge. (C-027)

## Stated assumptions (unresolved in debate)
- Settlement SLO target 150ms at 4× volume is unverified.
   Instrument it from day one; do not design around it. (C-031)
- Logical separation of card data is assumed acceptable to the
   regulator. Confirm before launch. (C-014)

## Extraction triggers (do not pre-build)
Platform headcount ≥ 4 FTE · settlement p99 > 150ms sustained ·
regulator requires physical separation.

## Deliverables
- modules/ ledger, settlement, cardvault, ledger-api
- contracts/ typed interfaces + import-graph rules
- ops/ circuit breakers, kill switch, alert routing…

02Shared component kit

One vocabulary under all six layouts

Whichever shell wins, these primitives stay identical. They encode the spec’s terminology directly — outcome states, evidence types, position verbs — so a user who learns them on the board can read them on the docket.

Semantic channels

agreed disputed unresolved dissent action

Four states, four colours, never reused for anything else. Accent cobalt is reserved for interaction so it can’t be mistaken for a verdict.

Outcome badge

consensusmajority w/ dissentsplit decisioninsufficient evidence

All four are terminal, equal-weight states. None is styled as an error or a partial result.

Evidence chip

factassumptioninferenceopinionunverified

Outline-only, never filled — evidence type qualifies a claim, it doesn’t rank it. Always sits with the claim ID.

Confidence meter

Ten segments, not a smooth bar. Discrete units resist being read as precision the model doesn’t have.

Claim card anatomy

C-0nntype
Claim text, always the largest thing on the card.
n/5 · evidence

ID, type, text, alignment dots, strength. Model dots are 7px — deliberately smaller than the claim.

Position verbs

heldmodifiedwithdrawnanswering

What a model did after being challenged is the most interesting fact about it — more than its score.

Cost ticker

$0.31of $2.00

Present on every screen where a model can be called. Turns amber past 60%, and the orchestrator stops before it turns red.

Type roles

Source Serif — claims, arguments, decision prose. Anything a model said.

Archivo — interface chrome, headings, buttons. Anything the product says.

IBM Plex Mono — IDs, scores, costs, event names. Anything the system measures.

03Recommendation

What I’d build for the 3–4 week MVP

Shell: D, then A

Ship Focus Flow first — it covers screens 1, 2, 3 and 5 with one column and no layout risk, and it forces the cost and progress story to be legible. Add the Control Room as a “pro view” toggle once the event contract is stable.

Decision surface: B

The Docket is the artefact people will share, and it is the cheapest to build — it renders the decision object directly. Its citations give you screen 4 for free before the board exists.

Defer: C’s graph

Build the board columns and the alignment matrix; leave the contradiction map for post-MVP. The map needs graph layout work that buys no information the matrix lacks.

E and F as-is

Both are single-purpose and small. The Workbench’s traceability counters are worth building on day one — they’re your regression test for spec-generation quality.

Week 1–2

D (screens 1–3) + live event plumbing + cost ticker

Week 3

B decision record + claim citations (screen 4 via drill-down)

Week 4

E Build Studio tabs + F history table

Post-MVP

A pro shell · C board and matrix · adaptive rounds

Not built here. These are layout studies — static HTML, invented content, no product code. Numbers, claims and model names are illustrative and chosen to stress the layouts, not to represent any real benchmark.
